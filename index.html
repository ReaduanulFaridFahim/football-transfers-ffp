The Manchester City FFP Case: Why They Got Banned & The 115 Charges

Manchester City is at the center of the largest financial fair play dispute in sports history. Between 2008 and 2026, the club spent over €2.48 Billion on player transfers, running a staggering €1.50 Billion net transfer deficit. Regulatory bodies investigated allegations that owner equity was disguised as commercial sponsorship to artificially inflate revenues and bypass UEFA and Premier League spending limits.

Chronology: From Football Leaks to European Ban & 115 Charges

Key legal milestones and rulings in Manchester City's financial fair play battles

November 2018
📰 The Football Leaks Revelations

German magazine Der Spiegel published leaked internal club emails alleging owner Sheikh Mansour's holding company (ADUG) was secretly funding £59.5M of Etihad's £67.5M sponsorship deal.

February 2020
🚫 UEFA Issues 2-Year Champions League Ban

UEFA's Club Financial Control Body (CFCB) found Man City guilty of "serious breaches" of FFP, issuing a 2-year ban from European competitions and a €30M fine.

July 2020
⚖️ CAS Overturns Ban on "Time-Barred" Technicality

The Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) overturned the ban because UEFA's 5-year statute of limitations expired on the older emails. CAS still upheld a €10M fine for non-cooperation.

February 2023 - Present
⚡ Premier League Charges 115 Breaches

The Premier League referred Man City to an independent commission with 115 alleged rule violations spanning 9 seasons. Crucially, Premier League rules have NO statute of limitations.

💡 Why Did This Trigger Regulatory Bans? (The Core FFP Principle)

Under UEFA Financial Fair Play (FFP) and Premier League Profitability and Sustainability Rules (PSR), clubs can only spend money that they earn organically through matchday tickets, TV broadcasting rights, and legitimate commercial sponsorship. If owners channel private wealth into their clubs by disguising it as sponsor revenue from affiliated state entities, the club appears to have much higher revenues than it actually generates. This allows them to outbid, outpay, and outspend competitor clubs on players while evading FFP deficits, fundamentally distorting sporting integrity.
