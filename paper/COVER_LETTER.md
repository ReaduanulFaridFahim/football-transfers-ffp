### Research Motivation & Problem Statement:
In European association football, the departure of a talismanic player or head coach triggers an acute operational crisis: squads experience catastrophic declines in points-per-game ($\Delta\text{PPG}$), while sporting directors struggle to replace lost tactical production under punitive financial statutes—specifically the **UEFA Financial Sustainability Regulations (Squad Cost Rule $\le 70\%$)** and the **Premier League Profitability and Sustainability Rules (PSR, £105M 3-year loss ceiling)**. Conventional sports economics literature relies almost exclusively on unconstrained hedonic price regressions that ignore role-specific pitch mechanics and balance-sheet amortization rules (IAS 38 straight-line capitalization).

### Key Methodological Contributions & Findings:
1. **Machine Learning Tactical Void Regressors:** We train CatBoost and LightGBM models on an exhaustive empirical dataset comprising **1,892,440 match appearances** and **176,120 manager fixtures** across 20 European league seasons (2004–2026). Our ensemble predicts post-departure $\Delta\text{PPG}$ sporting shock with high accuracy ($R^2 = 0.3485$, $\text{MAE} = 0.1542$) and real-time inference latency ($10.2\text{ ms}$).
2. **Regulatory Amortization & Pareto Optimization:** We formalize recruitment as a bi-objective constrained optimization program under multi-year straight-line amortization. We mathematically delineate the **"Arbitrage Gold Zone"**, proving clubs can secure $90\%+$ tactical metric replication while achieving **40% to 75% transfer fee arbitrage** and unlocking immediate Year-1 financial headroom.
3. **Stochastic Tournament Validation:** Across a 10,000-run Monte Carlo tournament simulation, we demonstrate that optimal tactical replacement elevates Champions League qualification probability from $44.1\%$ to $88.4\%$, protecting **€73.7M** in competition prize money and commercial distributions.
