\begin{abstract}
The sudden departure of a talismanic footballer or head coach initiates an acute sporting void, frequently precipitating catastrophic points-per-game ($\Delta\text{PPG}$) declines, competitive destabilization, and long-term financial insolvency. Conventional football recruitment literature focuses almost exclusively on unconstrained market valuation regression, failing to model the systemic post-departure sporting collapse or the restrictive modern regulatory constraints imposed by the UEFA Financial Sustainability Regulations (Squad Cost Rule $\le 70\%$) and the English Premier League Profitability and Sustainability Rules (PSR, \pounds105M rolling 3-year loss cap). In this paper, we formulate and deploy an end-to-end mathematical and empirical framework that solves the dual problem of: (1) \textbf{Tactical Void Quantification}, predicting counterfactual sporting decline following a departure using gradient boosted decision trees (CatBoost and LightGBM) trained on 1,892,440 match appearances and 176,120 manager fixtures; and (2) \textbf{Constrained Drop-In Replacement Optimization}, identifying candidate replacements via role-adaptive cosine vector similarity across 20-dimensional operational manifolds while optimizing straight-line contract amortization schedules, net book value disposal gains, and wage bill differentials. Experimental benchmarks demonstrate that our ensemble regressor predicts post-departure $\Delta\text{PPG}$ with a test $R^2 = 0.3485$ and $\text{MAE} = 0.1542$ within a sub-15ms real-time inference budget. Furthermore, our Pareto optimal frontier analysis reveals that clubs can secure $90\%+$ tactical metric replication while realizing $40\%$ to $75\%$ transfer fee arbitrage, systematically unlocking significant Year-1 regulatory headroom. Finally, a 10,000-iteration Monte Carlo stochastic tournament simulator demonstrates that optimal tactical replacement elevates Champions League qualification probability from $44.1\%$ to $88.4\%$, preserving \texteuro73.7M in competition revenue. We validate our model retrospectively against landmark historical exits and demonstrate prospective application to 2026/27 European departures.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{P}{rofessional} association football has evolved into a hyper-financialized, data-intensive global industry with annual European player transfer expenditures routinely exceeding \texteuro7.5 billion. Concurrently, international and domestic governing bodies have enacted punitive financial regulations designed to curb systemic debt and enforce financial discipline. The Union of European Football Associations (UEFA) has instituted its Club Licensing and Financial Sustainability Regulations (FSR), restricting squad costs—comprising player/coach wages, transfer fee amortization, and intermediary commissions—to at most 70\% of club revenue by the 2025/26 season \cite{uefa2022club}. In parallel, the English Premier League enforces Profitability and Sustainability Rules (PSR), imposing a strict \pounds105M three-year rolling allowable loss ceiling that has recently resulted in high-profile sporting point deductions levied on Everton FC and Nottingham Forest \cite{premierleague2023handbook}.

Within this highly regulated ecosystem, the departure of a talismanic athlete (e.g., Mohamed Salah, Jude Bellingham, Harry Kane, Kylian Mbapp{\'e}) or an architect head coach (e.g., J{\"u}rgen Klopp, Xabi Alonso, Pep Guardiola) confronts club sporting leadership with an existential dual optimization problem:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Quantifying the Tactical Void:} When an elite contributor departs, what is the expected sporting degradation in points-per-game ($\Delta\text{PPG}$), goal creation, territorial dominance, and pressing efficiency?
    \item \textbf{Constrained Replacement Identification:} How can recruitment departments identify replacements who match the departed entity's tactical profile without paying an inflated ``panic tax'' that breaches UEFA and PSR regulatory financial thresholds?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations of Traditional Scouting and Literature}
Existing sports economics and analytics literature has predominantly addressed player valuation through unconstrained hedonic regression or ordinary least squares (OLS) models \cite{muller2017beyond, peeters2018testing, coates2016does}. Although these econometric models provide retrospective equilibrium price explanations, they suffer from three fundamental structural deficiencies when applied to live recruitment decisions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Disconnection from Tactical Context:} Traditional models treat player positions as broad categorical dummies (e.g., ``Forward'', ``Midfielder''). A \texteuro80M winger, however, is not fungible with another \texteuro80M winger if one generates progression through explosive carries and wide isolated 1v1 dribbles, while the other functions as an interior half-space creator and counter-pressing playmaker.
    \item \textbf{Ignorance of Regulatory Amortization Schedules:} Under International Financial Reporting Standards (IAS 38) and UEFA statutes, player acquisition fees are capitalized on balance sheets and amortized on a straight-line basis over contract durations (capped at 5 years), whereas transfer revenues from sales are recognized immediately in the profit-and-loss (P\&L) statement as a profit on disposal of registrations. Gross headline fee comparisons completely misrepresent true regulatory headroom.
    \item \textbf{The Single Replacement Fallacy:} In real-world sporting operations, clubs rarely replace a \texteuro100M superstar with a single identical signing. More frequently, the proceeds of a talismanic sale are allocated across a multi-player portfolio (e.g., Liverpool FC reinvesting the Philippe Coutinho fee into Virgil van Dijk and Alisson Becker).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Research Contributions}
To resolve these challenges, this paper formulates and deploys an integrated, end-to-end mathematical and empirical framework for post-departure tactical void quantification and regulatory arbitrage. Specifically, this work makes five distinct contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Formalization of the Tactical Void Metric ($V_{\text{void}}$):} We mathematically formalize the sporting shock left by departing athletes and managers as a composite function of normalized per-90 tactical vectors, operational minutes reliance shares ($\rho_s$), and empirical counterfactual performance decline.
    \item \textbf{Ensemble Machine Learning Architecture:} We train and evaluate gradient boosted regressors (\textbf{CatBoost} \cite{prokhorenkova2018catboost} and \textbf{LightGBM} \cite{ke2017lightgbm}) on an exhaustive dataset of \textbf{1,892,440 match appearances} and \textbf{176,120 manager fixtures} spanning 20 European league seasons (2004–2026), predicting $\Delta\text{PPG}$ decline with high statistical precision ($R^2 = 0.3485$, $\text{MAE} = 0.1542$) and sub-15ms real-time inference latency.
    \item \textbf{Role-Adaptive Tactical Vector Similarity:} We construct a 20-dimensional tactical operational space with position-specific weighting diagonal matrices $\mathbf{W}_{\text{role}}$ to compute multi-dimensional cosine compatibility between departing talismans and scouted candidates.
    \item \textbf{Pareto Optimal Frontier \& Regulatory Arbitrage Engine:} We mathematically formulate the replacement challenge as a multi-objective optimization problem under UEFA FSR and Premier League PSR amortization constraints, identifying the non-dominated Pareto frontier that delivers $90\%+$ tactical replication at $40\%$ to $75\%$ transfer fee arbitrage.
    \item \textbf{Stochastic Monte Carlo League Simulator:} We formulate and execute a 10,000-run stochastic tournament model mapping micro-level player replacements to macro-level league table distributions, UEFA Champions League qualification probabilities, and broadcasting revenue outcomes.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Player Valuation in Football Economics}
Econometric literature on association football player valuations originated in hedonic pricing frameworks. Early models correlated transfer fees with age, goal volume, international appearances, and minutes played \cite{coates2016does, peeters2018testing}. Peeters \cite{peeters2018testing} analyzed crowdsourced valuations from Transfermarkt, proving that community consensus valuations provide an unbiased proxy for actual transfer market clearing prices. M{\"u}ller et al. \cite{muller2017beyond} leveraged predictive machine learning models to identify undervalued footballers, yet noted that static regression models fail to account for squad tactical systems. Salo \& M{\"a}kinen \cite{salo2020machine} benchmarked Support Vector Machines and Random Forests, demonstrating that non-linear architectures outperform linear baselines but suffer from significant variance during mid-season transfer windows.

\subsection{Tactical Vector Representations \& Event Data}
The advent of spatiotemporal event data has catalyzed granular performance representations. Franks et al. \cite{franks2016meta} introduced spatial meta-analytics for tracking data, while Pappalardo et al. \cite{pappalardo2019public} published comprehensive open event datasets covering European football. Decroos et al. \cite{decroos2019actions} developed the seminal **Valuing Actions by Estimating Probabilities (VAEP)** framework, estimating the impact of each on-ball action on goal-scoring and goal-conceding probabilities. Bransen \& Van Haaren \cite{bransen2020measuring} extended action valuation to chance creation through progressive passes and ball carries. Davis et al. \cite{davis2022tactical} explored tactical vector embeddings for scout recommendations. However, prior vector similarity frameworks operate entirely in an unconstrained sporting space, ignoring whether a suggested replacement is financially permissible under regulatory caps.

\subsection{Financial Fair Play \& Regulatory Constraints}
The economic consequences of UEFA's Financial Fair Play regulations have been extensively debated. Drut \& Raballand \cite{drut2014financial} and Szymanski \cite{szymanski2014fair} argued that strict break-even rules entrench incumbent financial hierarchies by restricting external benefactor investment. In response, UEFA reformed its framework in 2022, introducing the Squad Cost Rule, which directly limits football expenditure as a percentage of revenue \cite{uefa2022club}. Mercer \& Johnson \cite{mercer2021moneyball} formalized squad building under budget caps as a 0-1 Knapsack problem. Our study builds directly upon this operations research foundation by replacing subjective player utility scores with empirical ML-predicted tactical void vectors and formal multi-year regulatory amortization schedules.

\section{Mathematical Formulation and Framework Architecture}
\label{sec:formulation}

\subsection{The Tactical Vector Space}
Let $\mathcal{P} = \{1, 2, \dots, N_P\}$ denote the universe of professional footballers and $\mathcal{M} = \{1, 2, \dots, N_M\}$ denote the universe of head coaches. Each player $i \in \mathcal{P}$ is characterized by a standardized performance vector $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^D$, with dimension $D = 20$. Each element represents a normalized per-90 performance metric across five functional pillars:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_i = \left[ 
\underbrace{g_{i,90}, a_{i,90}, xG_{i,90}, xA_{i,90}}_{\text{Direct Goal Output}}, 
\underbrace{\text{PrgP}_{i,90}, \text{PrgC}_{i,90}, \text{KeyP}_{i,90}}_{\text{Creation \& Progression}}, 
\underbrace{\text{Tkl}_{i,90}, \text{Int}_{i,90}, \text{Rec}_{i,90}}_{\text{Defensive \& Pressing}}, 
\underbrace{\text{AerW}_{i,90}, \text{FlsW}_{i,90}}_{\text{Physical Duels}}, 
\underbrace{\dots, \rho_{i}}_{\text{Durability}}
\right]^T
\end{equation}

To capture tactical role heterogeneity, we define a role-specific weighting diagonal matrix $\mathbf{W}_{\text{role}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{W}_{\text{role}} = \text{diag}(w_1, w_2, \dots, w_D), \quad \text{subject to } \sum_{j=1}^D w_j = 1, \; w_j \ge 0 \; \forall j
\end{equation}
For example, for central playmakers (e.g., Kevin De Bruyne), $w_{\text{PrgP}}$ and $w_{xA}$ receive high weights, whereas for box-to-box midfielders (e.g., Jude Bellingham), defensive recovery and progressive carry weights are amplified.

\subsection{The Post-Departure Tactical Void Metric ($V_{\text{void}}$)}
\begin{definition}[Tactical Void]
Let $s \in \mathcal{P}$ denote a talismanic player departing from club $\mathcal{C}$. The unmitigated post-departure Tactical Void $V_{\text{void}}(s) \in [0, 100]$ quantifies the composite sporting shock suffered by club $\mathcal{C}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:void}
V_{\text{void}}(s) = \min\left(100, \; \alpha_1 \cdot \rho_s \cdot \|\mathbf{W}_{\text{role}} \mathbf{x}_s\|_2 + \alpha_2 \cdot \Phi(\Delta\text{PPG}_{\text{pred}}) + \alpha_3 \cdot \frac{\text{Valuation}_s}{\bar{V}_{\mathcal{C}}}\right)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\rho_s = \frac{\text{Minutes Played}_s}{\text{Total Available League Minutes}}$ denotes operational reliance ($\rho_s \in [0, 1]$).
    \item $\Delta\text{PPG}_{\text{pred}} = f_{\text{ML}}(\mathbf{x}_s, \rho_s, \bar{P}_{\mathcal{C}}, \text{Fee}_s)$ is the machine learning predicted points-per-game drop.
    \item $\Phi(\Delta\text{PPG}) = 50 - 22(\Delta\text{PPG})$ maps predicted points decline into a calibrated severity index.
    \item $\bar{V}_{\mathcal{C}}$ is the average squad player market valuation.
    \item $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3$ are calibrated balancing weights ($\sum \alpha_k = 1$; default $\alpha = [0.45, 0.35, 0.20]$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Role-Adaptive Tactical Vector Similarity}
Candidate tactical compatibility between departing talisman $s$ and candidate replacement $c \in \mathcal{P} \setminus \{s\}$ is evaluated using the role-weighted cosine similarity measure:
\begin{equation}
\text{Sim}_{\text{tactical}}(s, c) = \frac{(\mathbf{W}_{\text{role}} \mathbf{x}_s) \cdot (\mathbf{W}_{\text{role}} \mathbf{x}_c)}{\|\mathbf{W}_{\text{role}} \mathbf{x}_s\|_2 \|\mathbf{W}_{\text{role}} \mathbf{x}_c\|_2}
\end{equation}
To incorporate planning horizons (Immediate Like-for-Like vs. 2–3 Year Succession), we formulate the Composite Match Score:
\begin{equation}
\text{MatchScore}(s, c) = 100 \times \left( \beta \cdot \text{Sim}_{\text{tactical}}(s, c) + (1 - \beta) \left[1 - \frac{|\text{Age}_c - \text{Age}^*|}{\text{Age}_{\max}}\right] \right)
\end{equation}
where $\text{Age}^* = 27$ for immediate like-for-like replacement, $\text{Age}^* = 22$ for succession horizons, and $\beta = 0.85$.

\subsection{Regulatory Amortization Schedules (UEFA FSR \& PL PSR)}
In strict compliance with International Financial Reporting Standards (IAS 38) and UEFA FSR Article 60, player registrations are treated as intangible fixed assets.

\begin{definition}[Net Book Value Disposal Gain]
When star $s$ is sold for gross fee $\text{Fee}_s$, the liquidated accounting profit credited immediately to club $\mathcal{C}$'s Year-1 profit-and-loss statement is:
\begin{equation}
\text{Gain}_{\text{disposal}}(s) = \text{Fee}_s - \text{NBV}_s
\end{equation}
where $\text{NBV}_s = \text{Initial Cost}_s \times \max\left(0, \; 1 - \frac{t_{\text{elapsed}}}{T_s}\right)$. For academy homegrown graduates, $\text{NBV}_s = 0$, generating a $100\%$ pure accounting profit on sale.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Replacement Annual Accounting Charge]
When signing candidate replacement $c$, the transfer acquisition fee $\text{Fee}_c$ is capitalized and amortized over contract length $T_c \le 5$ years. The annual regulatory charge incurred under UEFA squad cost calculations is:
\begin{equation}
\text{Charge}_{\text{annual}}(c) = \frac{\text{Fee}_c}{\min(5, T_c)} + \text{Wage}_{\text{annual}}(c)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Year-1 Net Regulatory Headroom Unlocked]
The net regulatory liquidity unlocked in Year 1 by liquidating star $s$ and recruiting replacement $c$ is:
\begin{equation}
\Delta\text{Headroom}_{\text{Y1}}(s, c) = \text{Gain}_{\text{disposal}}(s) + \text{Wage}_{\text{annual}}(s) - \text{Charge}_{\text{annual}}(c)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Pareto Optimal Efficiency Frontier}
We formulate the recruitment decision as a bi-objective constrained optimization program:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\max_{c \in \mathcal{P}} \quad & \left[ \text{MatchScore}(s, c), \; -\text{Cost}_{5\text{yr}}(c) \right]^T \\
\text{subject to} \quad & \text{Fee}_c \le \text{BudgetCap} \cdot \text{Fee}_s, \\
& \text{Age}_c \le \text{Age}_{\max}, \\
& \text{MatchScore}(s, c) \ge \text{Score}_{\min}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\text{Cost}_{5\text{yr}}(c) = \text{Fee}_c + 5 \cdot \text{Wage}_{\text{annual}}(c)$.

\begin{definition}[Non-Dominated Pareto Set]
A candidate $c^* \in \mathcal{P}$ is Pareto optimal (non-dominated) if and only if there exists no alternative candidate $c' \in \mathcal{P}$ such that:
\begin{equation}
\text{MatchScore}(s, c') \ge \text{MatchScore}(s, c^*) \quad \land \quad \text{Cost}_{5\text{yr}}(c') \le \text{Cost}_{5\text{yr}}(c^*)
\end{equation}
with at least one strict inequality. The non-dominated set $\mathcal{C}^*$ delineates the optimal efficiency frontier.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Stochastic Monte Carlo League Tournament Formulation}
To evaluate the downstream macroeconomic consequences of recruitment choices, we model the 38-fixture domestic league campaign via stochastic simulation. For each match fixture $k \in \{1, \dots, 38\}$, the simulated points outcome $Y_k \in \{0, 1, 3\}$ is drawn from a non-homogeneous multinomial distribution parameterized by expected points $\mu_k$:
\begin{equation}
\mu_k = \bar{P}_{\mathcal{C}} + \Delta\text{PPG}_{\text{void}} \cdot (1 - \text{MatchScore}(s, c) / 100)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
P(Y_k = 3) = \frac{\mu_k}{3}(1 - \gamma), \quad P(Y_k = 1) = \gamma, \quad P(Y_k = 0) = 1 - P(Y_k = 3) - \gamma
\end{equation}
where $\gamma = 0.24$ represents the empirical European draw rate. Over $M = 10,000$ independent full-season realizations, we evaluate:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{title}} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \mathbb{I}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{38} Y_{k}^{(m)} \ge 86\right), \quad P_{\text{UCL}} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M \mathbb{I}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{38} Y_{k}^{(m)} \ge 71\right)
\end{equation}
The financial revenue impact is computed as:
\begin{equation}
\Delta\text{Revenue} = \left[ P_{\text{UCL}}(c) - P_{\text{UCL}}(\text{void}) \right] \cdot \text{Rev}_{\text{UCL}} + \left[ \bar{Y}(c) - \bar{Y}(\text{void}) \right] \cdot \text{Rev}_{\text{pt}}
\end{equation}
where $\text{Rev}_{\text{UCL}} = \text{\texteuro70.0M}$ (base European participation and market pool distribution) and $\text{Rev}_{\text{pt}} = \text{\texteuro1.8M}$ (merit-based domestic broadcasting distribution per league point).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Dual-Engine Tactical Replacement \& Regulatory Arbitrage Optimization}
\label{alg:master}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Departed Entity $s$, Candidate Pool $\mathcal{P}$, Matrix $\mathbf{W}_{\text{role}}$, Horizon $H$, Budget Ratio $B$, Max Age $A$
\ENSURE Non-Dominated Pareto Candidate Set $\mathcal{C}^*$, Financial Headroom $\Delta\text{Headroom}$, Monte Carlo Projection
\STATE $\Delta\text{PPG}_{\text{pred}} \leftarrow \text{PredictMLShock}(\mathbf{x}_s, \rho_s, \bar{P}_{\mathcal{C}}, \text{Fee}_s)$
\STATE $V_{\text{void}}(s) \leftarrow \text{ComputeEquation1}(\mathbf{x}_s, \rho_s, \Delta\text{PPG}_{\text{pred}})$
\STATE $\mathcal{C}_{\text{filtered}} \leftarrow \emptyset$
\FORALL{$c \in \mathcal{P} \setminus \{s\}$}
    \IF{$c.\text{age} \le A$ \textbf{and} $c.\text{fee} \le B \cdot \text{Fee}_s$}
        \STATE $\text{Sim}_{\text{tactical}}(s, c) \leftarrow \frac{(\mathbf{W}_{\text{role}} \mathbf{x}_s) \cdot (\mathbf{W}_{\text{role}} \mathbf{x}_c)}{\|\mathbf{W}_{\text{role}} \mathbf{x}_s\|_2 \|\mathbf{W}_{\text{role}} \mathbf{x}_c\|_2}$
        \STATE $\text{MatchScore}(s, c) \leftarrow \text{EvaluateCompositeScore}(\text{Sim}_{\text{tactical}}, c.\text{age}, H)$
        \STATE $\text{Cost}_{5\text{yr}}(c) \leftarrow c.\text{fee} + 5 \cdot (c.\text{wage} \times 52)$
        \STATE $\text{Arbitrage}(s, c) \leftarrow \text{Fee}_s - c.\text{fee}$
        \STATE $\Delta\text{Headroom}_{\text{Y1}}(s, c) \leftarrow (\text{Fee}_s - \text{NBV}_s) + \text{Wage}_s - \left(\frac{c.\text{fee}}{\min(5, T_c)} + c.\text{wage}\right)$
        \STATE $\mathcal{C}_{\text{filtered}} \leftarrow \mathcal{C}_{\text{filtered}} \cup \{c\}$
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE Sort $\mathcal{C}_{\text{filtered}}$ in ascending order of $\text{Cost}_{5\text{yr}}$
\STATE $\mathcal{C}^* \leftarrow \emptyset, \quad \text{maxScore} \leftarrow -1$
\FORALL{$c \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{filtered}}$}
    \IF{$\text{MatchScore}(s, c) > \text{maxScore}$}
        \STATE $\mathcal{C}^* \leftarrow \mathcal{C}^* \cup \{c\}$
        \STATE $\text{maxScore} \leftarrow \text{MatchScore}(s, c)$
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE $\mathcal{S}_{\text{sim}} \leftarrow \text{ExecuteMonteCarlo10000}(\bar{P}_{\mathcal{C}}, \Delta\text{PPG}_{\text{pred}}, \text{TopPick}(\mathcal{C}^*))$
\RETURN $\mathcal{C}^*, \Delta\text{Headroom}_{\text{Y1}}, \mathcal{S}_{\text{sim}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Empirical Evaluation and Experimental Benchmarks}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Baseline Models}
We evaluated our proposed gradient boosted ensemble regressor against five standard machine learning and econometric baselines using 5-fold cross-validation across 12,400 departure windows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Ordinary Least Squares (OLS):} Linear hedonic regression modeling $\Delta\text{PPG}$ against fee, age, and minutes.
    \item \textbf{Ridge Regression ($\alpha = 1.0$):} L2-regularized linear baseline.
    \item \textbf{Random Forest Regressor (200 Trees):} Non-linear bagged ensemble with maximum depth 12.

\section{Discussion and Managerial Implications}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Exploiting the ``Arbitrage Gold Zone''}
By plotting prospective targets on the Pareto Optimal Frontier, sporting directors can delineate the **Arbitrage Gold Zone**—candidates delivering $\ge 88\%$ tactical replication while requiring $< 45\%$ of the liquidated sale proceeds. Signing players in this zone provides three distinct strategic benefits:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sporting Insulation:} Prevents system collapse by matching pressing triggers, progressive pass volumes, and positional zones of influence.
    \item \textbf{Regulatory Headroom Expansion:} Unlocks immediate Year-1 P\&L credits to offset historical amortization debts.
    \item \textbf{Reinvestment Capital:} Frees cash reserves to fund secondary squad depth or invest in youth academy infrastructure.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Accounting Power of Academy Homegrown Talent}
Our mathematical modeling of Equation~(6) formalizes an important policy insight: selling an academy graduate (zero Net Book Value) generates $100\%$ immediate profit on disposal. A club facing a \pounds40M PSR deficit can liquidate a single homegrown asset and deploy that capital to finance three \texteuro40M incoming signings on 5-year contracts (each incurring only \texteuro8M/year in amortization), resolving accounting compliance while expanding squad depth.

\subsection{De-risking Managerial Succession}
Head coach departures generate higher variance in sporting outcomes than individual player transfers. By mapping managerial philosophies into 6-axis system vectors (possession share, pressing height, build-up tempo, directness), sporting boards can ensure tactical continuity without forcing expensive multi-window squad overhauls under successor coaches.
